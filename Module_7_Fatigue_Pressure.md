# Module 7: Fatigue, Pressure, & Stop-Work Authority

**Core Philosophy:** The greatest risk on a 2:00 AM load-out isn't the machine — it's the exhausted "Hero Operator" who ignores their own limits.

### [SCENARIO: THE 4:00 AM PUSH]
It is 4:00 AM. You’ve been on the sticks for 14 hours. Your vision is blurring. The Tour Manager is screaming to finish the load.

**Question:** What is your immediate action when asked to make one last risky lift?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Calls "STOP", communicates fatigue, and requests relief.

**AI Response:**  
"Correct. You demonstrated real **Stop-Work Authority**. Admitting fatigue is not weakness — it is professionalism. You protected the crew and the tour from a preventable accident."

#### Wrong Path
**Trainee:** Tries to push through to "save the show."

**AI Response:**  
"STOP. You just became the highest risk factor on site. Your delayed reaction time would have turned a simple lift into a dropped load or injury. The schedule is not worth a career-ending incident."

**Tour Reality:** A Tour Manager might be angry for ten minutes, but they will be devastated forever if you have an accident. Use your Stop-Work Authority.
