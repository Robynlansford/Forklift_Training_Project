# Module 5: Night Load-In Chaos & Sensory Overload

**Core Philosophy:** At 2:00 AM, the greatest hazard is not the machine — it is your own compromised senses. Fatigue, flash blindness, and low visibility turn routine moves into high-risk events.

### [SCENARIO: THE STROBE FLASH BLACKOUT]
It is 1:15 AM. You are backing a wide stack of audio amplifier racks out of the stage left wing. Suddenly, the lighting crew tests high-intensity strobes directly above you. A brilliant white flash completely wipes your night vision.

**Question:** What is your immediate action?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Immediately stops the machine, calls "STOP", and waits for vision to return.

**AI Response:**  
"Outstanding discipline. You deployed the **STOP Rule** the instant your vision was compromised. Continuing to move while blind is one of the fastest ways to cause a crush injury or drop expensive gear. You held position until your eyes recovered — the professional move."

#### Wrong Path
**Trainee:** Keeps moving slowly or tries to lean out to see.

**AI Response:**  
"Stop! Catastrophic error. While blind, the amp stack drifted and smashed into a low-hanging cable bundle, severing a $15,000 fiber line. When flash blindness hits, your velocity must drop to zero instantly. Never trust muscle memory in the dark."

### [SCENARIO: TIRE SCRUBBING ON ARENA FLOOR]
You are on a protected NBA arena hardwood court covered with temporary plywood. You need to make a tight turn with a heavy barricade cart.

**Question:** How do you execute the turn without damaging the floor?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Maintains slow rolling momentum instead of stationary spinning.

**AI Response:**  
"Correct. You avoided **Tire Scrubbing**. Stationary hard turns generate massive torsional friction that can burn through plywood and scar the expensive hardwood underneath. Rolling turns distribute the load safely."

### [SCENARIO: ICE RINK INSULATION TRAP]
You are on a dual-purpose arena with temporary insulated decking over sub-surface ice. You are holding a 6,000 lb stack of steel base plates in the center for 15 minutes.

**Question:** What risk do you consider?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Recognizes compression/sagging risk and moves the load to solid perimeter or keeps the machine moving.

**AI Response:**  
"Excellent awareness. That is the **Ice Rink Insulation Trap**. Heavy stationary loads cause the panels to compress and cold-sweat into the ice, creating a subtle dip that tilts your mast and destabilizes the machine. Never park heavy concentrated loads long-term on temporary ice decking."

### Core Drills Summary
1. **Flash Blindness + STOP Rule**
2. **Tire Scrubbing Prevention**
3. **Ice Rink / Temporary Deck Awareness**
4. **Fatigue Recognition in Low Light**

**Tour Reality:** At 2:00 AM, your senses are your weakest link. The best operators know when to stop, look up, and protect both the gear and the crew.
