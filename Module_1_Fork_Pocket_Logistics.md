# Module 1: Truck Pack & Fork Pocket Logistics

**Core Philosophy:** In touring production, every inch in the truck counts. A perfect pack means the doors close and the gear arrives safely. One bad pack can delay the entire load-in or damage expensive equipment.

### [SCENARIO: TIGHT TRUCK PACK]
You are loading a custom lighting pod cart into a 53-foot production semi at night. The crew has packed the previous row tightly. The swivel casters on your cart are turned sideways under pressure.

**Question:** How do you get the casters to trail straight so the cart fits flush without damaging neighboring cases?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Uses the micro-back-off / jiggle technique.

**AI Response:**  
"Perfect. That is the **Caster-Spin Alignment** technique. By gently backing off one inch after contact, you allow the swivel wheels to straighten and trail parallel. You just saved critical inches and prevented damage to the adjacent cart."

#### Wrong Path
**Trainee:** Rams it or pushes hard.

**AI Response:**  
"Stop! That brute force will buckle caster mounts or splinter the plywood skin of the neighboring case. On a tour, you don’t force it — you work with the geometry. Try again: the wheels are sideways — what micro-movement do you make?"

### [SCENARIO: BREAKING FRICTION ON EXTRACTION]
The cart is now tightly packed. You need to pull it out the next day.

**Question:** How do you break the side-to-side friction without pulling neighboring gear with it?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Uses side-shift to wiggle horizontally first.

**AI Response:**  
"Excellent. You used the **Side-Shift** function to break the friction grip. This prevents the neighboring cart from lifting or tipping — a common and expensive mistake on load-outs."

### [SCENARIO: FLUSH-FORK RULE]
The cart is only 36 inches deep, but your forks are 48 inches long.

**Question:** How do you protect the ground crew behind the cart?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Leaves extra fork length visible on their side (Flush-Fork Rule).

**AI Response:**  
"Spot on. That is the **Flush-Fork Rule**. You intentionally leave the extra steel visible on your side so the ground crew isn’t at risk of being pinned by invisible fork tips."

**Tour Reality:** A perfect truck pack and clean fork work is what separates pros from crews that get sent home.
