# Module 6: Rigging Coordination & "Up-Look" Protocol

**Core Philosophy:** The airspace above the operator is a controlled, high-stakes workspace. The lift operator is a **Rigging Liaison**, not just a driver.

### [SCENARIO: 2:00 AM ARENA DROP]
You are backing a telehandler with a heavy audio array toward center stage. High-riggers are active 60 feet above. Visibility is low.

**Question:** What is your first action before crossing into the center-stage area?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Performs the Up-Look scan and confirms the drop-zone is clear.

**AI Response:**  
"Correct. You followed the **Up-Look Protocol**. A falling 2 lb shackle from 60 ft can strike with devastating force. By clearing the zone first, you protected both the crew and the production schedule."

#### Wrong Path
**Trainee:** Proceeds without scanning or calls a vague warning.

**AI Response:**  
"STOP. You entered the rigging zone without clearing it. A loose shackle just dropped from above and struck the load. Ignoring the airspace above you is one of the fastest ways to cause a serious injury or show-stopping damage."

### [SCENARIO: PUSHER BLINDNESS + RIGGING]
Ground crew is pushing a tall cart while riggers are working overhead.

**Question:** How do you coordinate both ground and overhead hazards?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Calls "STOP", clears the halo zone, and performs Up-Look before moving.

**AI Response:**  
"Outstanding. You managed both the **Pusher's Blindness** and the overhead rigging risk. You called 'STOP', confirmed the 3-foot halo was clear, and did a quick Up-Look before proceeding."

**Tour Reality:** Respect the air above you as much as the ground beneath you. One missed "Up-Look" can turn a routine lift into a catastrophe.
