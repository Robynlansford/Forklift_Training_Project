# Module 0: Basic Telehandler & Forklift Controls Review

**Core Philosophy:**  
Before you can safely handle expensive concert gear under pressure, you must first master the basic controls of the machine. This module assumes **zero prior knowledge** and explains everything in simple, step-by-step language.

### Safety First – Pre-Operation Inspection
Before starting any machine, perform this quick check:
- Tires / tracks in good condition and properly inflated.
- Forks or boom in good shape (no cracks or bent tips).
- Fluid leaks under the machine.
- Seat belt and mirrors functional.
- Horn and lights working.
- Parking brake holds the machine.

If anything looks wrong — **do not operate the machine**. Report it immediately.

### 1. Common Machines in Concert Production

**A. Sit-Down Counterbalance Forklift**  
Most common indoors (arenas, loading docks).  
4 wheels, vertical mast with forks. Typical capacity: 4,000–10,000 lbs.

**B. Rough-Terrain Forklift**  
Used outdoors on grass, dirt, or mud. Larger tires and higher ground clearance.

**C. Telehandler (Boom Forklift)**  
Most important for high lifts. Extendable boom instead of a vertical mast. Can reach 20–60+ feet high — ideal for flying truss, LED walls, and stage decks.

### 2. Getting In and Starting the Machine

1. Always approach from the **left side**.
2. Use steps and handrails — never jump.
3. Adjust the seat so your back is fully supported and you can reach all pedals comfortably.
4. Fasten your **seat belt** every single time.
5. Ensure the parking brake is engaged and the machine is in **Neutral**.
6. Start the engine and let it warm up for 30–60 seconds.

### 3. Main Controls (Sit-Down Forklift)

| Control                | What It Does                                      | How to Use It                                      |
|------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| Steering Wheel         | Turns the machine left or right                   | Turn like a car                                    |
| Accelerator Pedal      | Moves the machine forward or backward             | Gentle pressure with right foot                    |
| Brake Pedal            | Slows down or stops the machine                   | Right foot                                         |
| Parking Brake          | Locks the machine when parked                     | Lever or button                                    |
| Mast Lift Lever        | Raises and lowers the forks                       | Pull back = raise, push forward = lower            |
| Mast Tilt Lever        | Tilts forks forward or backward                   | Pull back = tilt back (safer when carrying loads)  |
| Side-Shift             | Moves forks left/right as a unit                  | Moves entire carriage sideways                     |
| Horn                   | Sounds the horn                                   | Button on steering wheel                           |

### 4. Telehandler-Specific Controls

- **Boom Lift** — Raises and lowers the entire boom.
- **Boom Extend / Retract** — Makes the boom longer or shorter (critical for reaching high trusses or stage decks).
- **Boom Tilt** — Tilts the forks at the end of the boom.
- **Outriggers / Stabilizers** — Extend from the sides to increase stability when lifting high or reaching far.
- **Steering Modes** — Switch between 2-wheel, 4-wheel, and crab steering.

### 5. Basic Operating Sequence

**Picking up a load:**
1. Approach straight on and stop about 1 foot away.
2. Lower forks to correct height and level them.
3. Drive forward slowly until forks are fully under the load.
4. Lift slightly, then tilt forks back a little for stability.
5. Reverse slowly.

**Traveling with a load:**
- Keep load low (4–8 inches off ground).
- Tilt forks slightly back.
- Drive slowly and always look in the direction of travel.

**Placing a load:**
1. Approach straight on.
2. Lower gently until load is stable.
3. Back away slowly.

### 6. Parking and Shutting Down

1. Lower forks completely to the ground.
2. Tilt forks forward slightly so they rest flat.
3. Shift to Neutral and engage parking brake.
4. Turn off the machine and remove the key.

**Tour Reality:**  
Basic controls must become automatic muscle memory. Once they are second nature, you can focus on the real challenges of touring — rigging above you, blind stagehands, wind, tight truck packs, and 2:00 AM fatigue.
