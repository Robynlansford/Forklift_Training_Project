# Capstone Scenario: The 2:00 AM Arena Load-Out

**Objective:** Successfully manage a high-stakes, multi-variable load-in under real production pressure and fatigue.

### [THE BRIEFING]
- **Time:** 2:00 AM.
- **Location:** Center Stage, Arena Floor.
- **Conditions:** Shift Duration: 14 hours. Ground: Muddy festival grass transitioning to concrete. Wind: 17 mph gusting. Task: Deploy 10,000 lb LED wall section. Rigging: Active high-riggers 60 ft above. Visibility: Low due to haze and dim lighting.

### [SCENARIO FLOW]

**Step 1: The Perimeter Approach**  
You approach the 20 ft rigging zone with the LED wall on the forks.  
**Question:** What is your first action?  

**Correct Action:** Perform a deliberate "Up-Look" scan and confirm the drop-zone is clear.  
**Engine Check:** If you skip the Up-Look, the TelehandlerSafetyEngine returns BLOCKER (overhead hazard).

**Step 2: The Wind & Load Challenge**  
As you begin to raise the boom, wind gusts hit 17 mph.  
**Question:** What do you do?  

**Correct Action:** Recognize the 15 mph threshold and call "STOP" to hold position.  
**Engine Check:** The engine returns HARD_STOP. Demonstrate Stop-Work Authority even under pressure.

**Step 3: The Pusher Blindness Test**  
Ground crew rushes in to guide the load.  
**Question:** What is your immediate action?  

**Correct Action:** Call "STOP", wait for the echo, confirm the 3-foot halo is clear, and verify fork pocket alignment before lowering.  
**Engine Check:** Moving before halo clearance triggers CAUTION (Accordion Effect / pusher blindness).

**Step 4: Fatigue Decision (New)**  
You feel a microsleep coming on after 14 hours. The Tour Manager pressures you to finish quickly.  
**Question:** What do you do?  

**Correct Action:** Call "STOP" and request relief.  
**Engine Check:** Continuing under fatigue would trigger a safety warning in a full system.

### [FINAL EVALUATION]
To pass, the operator must:
1. Always perform the "Up-Look" before entering rigging zones.
2. Never ignore a HARD_STOP (e.g., wind) regardless of pressure.
3. Always secure the "STOP" echo and clear the halo zone before any hand-off.
4. Recognize personal fatigue and use Stop-Work Authority.

**Tour Reality:** You aren’t just moving gear — you are managing the risk profile of the entire production. A true professional knows the safest move is often the one that stops the clock.
