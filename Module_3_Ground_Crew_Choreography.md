# Module 3: Ground Crew Choreography & Assembly-Line Safety

**Core Philosophy:** The forklift operator is not just driving — they are the **regulator** of a high-speed human assembly line. One mistimed move can crush a stagehand or destroy thousands of dollars in gear.

### [SCENARIO: THE DOCK UNLOAD]
You are backing a heavy lighting rack out of a 53-foot production semi at 1:45 AM. The moment your rear tires hit the dock plate, three local stagehands rush in behind the load to grab the case handles while your machine is still rolling in reverse.

**Question:** What is your mandatory first action before anyone touches the gear?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Brings the machine to a complete stop, shifts to neutral, lowers the load fully, and confirms "Clear-and-Release" before allowing contact.

**AI Response:**  
"Perfect execution. That is the **Clear-and-Release Pause**. You eliminated the dynamic crush hazard by bringing the forklift to a full structural stop and waiting until the casters are firmly on the deck before letting any hands engage. This is the standard that separates professionals from accidents."

#### Wrong Path
**Trainee:** Drops the load while still moving or expects the crew to time it.

**AI Response:**  
"Emergency freeze! By lowering the load while the machine still had backward momentum, you created an **Accordion Trap**. A stagehand tripped on the dock plate lip and the heavy case rolled back, pinning his leg against your chassis. Local crews focus on the gear — not your tire direction. You must control the pause."

### [SCENARIO: THE REAR-SWING TURN]
You need to make a sharp 90-degree turn on the loading dock. A group of local hands is clustered on your left flank, talking to a rigger.

**Question:** How do you handle the 3-Foot Rear-Swing Halo Zone?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Gives a short horn tap, waits for eye contact, and confirms the 3-foot halo is clear before turning.

**AI Response:**  
"Spot on. You respected the physics of counterbalance steering and accounted for **acoustic fatigue**. A short, deliberate horn tap + eye contact is far more effective than constant honking that creates 'horn blindness'."

### [SCENARIO: PUSHER'S BLINDNESS]
A crew of four stagehands is pushing a tall 8-foot video frame cart down a ramp directly toward your path.

**Question:** What do you do?

#### Correct Path
**Trainee:** Yields immediately and holds position until the blind pushers clear the intersection.

**AI Response:**  
"Outstanding. That is the **Pusher's Blindness Yield Rule**. Momentum beats right-of-way every time on a tour site. By stopping and letting them pass, you prevented a major collision."

### Core Drills Summary
1. **Clear-and-Release Pause**
2. **3-Foot Rear-Swing Halo**
3. **Pusher's Blindness Yield**
4. **"STOP" Echo under pressure**

**Tour Reality:** You are not just moving gear. You are the conductor of a dangerous human assembly line. One missed pause or blind turn can end a career or a show.
